# Terrain, movement and sight

- `settlePlates` (`app/game/pressurePlates.ts`) and `settleSignals` (`app/game/signals.ts`) each run one settle pass per call, not a loop to a fixed point. Plates or receivers wired to trigger each other oscillate once per tick instead of hanging a tick; a cascade resolves over the following ticks.
- A queued step must be takeable in the same tick the previous step commits (`GameSession.requestStep`). Otherwise every cell costs an extra tick and the server falls behind the client's prediction.
- `GameSession.advanceMotion` ends a walk with `reached` (`app/game/ticks.ts`), not a plain `>=`. `TICK_MS` is not exact, so twelve ticks add up to 399.99999999999994ms, and a 400ms step (a player wading in water, or the cat) would commit a tick after `RemoteSession` lands its prediction of the same step on the frame clock.
- A tile with a `footprint` is stored as one placement per covered cell: the anchor in the south-east cell and a part (carrying `span`) in each other cell. `settleSpans` (`app/lib/footprint.ts`) is the only writer of parts and runs on every assignment to `GameSession.map` and every editor `commitMap`. Removing any cell removes all of them; a change to the tile, facing or variant of any cell is copied to the rest and re-cuts the footprint; a change a part's cell cannot take (`canReplaceStack`) is undone in every cell. Because of the setter, a `GameSession` edit has to be derived from `this.map`, not from a value already assigned to it, or the parts it settled are lost.
- A part carries only `tileId`, `direction` and `variant`. Anything that reads a placement's other fields resolves a part to its anchor first (`spanAnchor`), as `pickTopAt`, `interact` and `canInteract` do. Renderers and the light bake skip parts, so the tile is drawn and lit once, from the anchor.
- Nothing moves a footprint yet: `pushDestination` refuses a column holding a `span`. Moving only the anchor would make `settleSpans` remove the old footprint and place a new one, and whether those overlap depends on the order they settle in.
- The world is an island, and the ring of `deep-water` round it is the only thing stopping a body walking off the edge of the map. Nothing past the last cell is solid ground, so a gap in the ring, or a pier or causeway built across it, is a way out of the world. The ring reaches at least 18 cells past the last cell anybody can stand in: `VIEW_CELLS` is 23, so that keeps the edge of the map off screen from the shallows. Open sea is a bare `deep-water` stack; only the cells next to the shallows have `water` under them, which is what shows through their faded edge.
- `MIN_LEVEL..MAX_LEVEL` (`app/lib/types.ts`) is how deep the world may go, not what a frame or a tick pays for. A walk over the whole map goes through `presentLevels(map)` (`app/lib/mapData.ts`), or `presentLevelsAndAbove` when it reads the stack on the level below, or `presentInEither` when it diffs two maps. Anything that draws, lights, sends or sight-tests walks `viewLevels(viewerZ)` (`app/lib/view.ts`). A new `for (let z = MIN_LEVEL; z <= MAX_LEVEL; z++)` makes its caller cost as much as the world is deep.
- `presentLevels` is cached on the `map.levels` record. Code that adds or removes a level key in place has to replace that record, as `setStacks` and the light worker's `applyMapPatch` do, or every later walk misses the new level.
- Every material built with `injectWorldShader` measures depth from its renderer's `DepthOriginUniforms`, which `depthOriginAt` sets each frame from the camera's centre and the viewer's level. A material given an origin that is never updated measures from the world's origin, and anything about twelve levels or 256 cells away from it clamps to the near or far plane and loses its draw order.
- `INTEREST_REACH_CELLS` (`app/net/interest.ts`) has to cover `WorldRenderer.lightWindow` plus the bake's apron. Both add `VIEW_LEVEL_REACH`, so raising it widens the subscription in the same edit; a light window wider than the subscription lights the cells the client was never sent as open sky.
- `MAX_STACK_HEIGHT` (`app/lib/types.ts`) is both the tallest stack `fitsInColumn` allows and the bound the column walks rely on: `standingSurfacesBetween`, `findLandingAbs`, `fitsHeightAtElevation`, `surfaceTileAt` and `climbFromSourceAt` read only the levels within it of the height they ask about. Letting a stack grow taller than it, in validation or in `removeUnfitPlacements`, makes those walks miss its top.
- Raised ground is a filled column: every level under the top holds the ground tile and two `half-stone`, and the stack that was on the ground sits on the top level. The ground tile under the fill is not decoration. `grass-ledge` and `sand-ledge` draw a rim towards every neighbour on their own level that holds neither the ground nor a ledge, so without it the foot of each cliff would be rimmed as well as its top.
- A ledge goes on every raised cell with a lower cell among its eight neighbours, directly above the ground tile. It is `intangible`, so the ground under it is still the surface a body stands on.
- Climbing one level takes two cells in a line: `ramp`, then `half-stone` with a `ramp` on it, both facing away from the cliff. A body cannot step sideways onto the upper of the two, so it blocks the foot of the cliff; `bun run raise:hills` only puts one where the cells beside it can be walked round through the lower.
- `bun run raise:hills` leaves the town alone because `HILLS.town` in `scripts/raise-hills.ts` holds its rectangle. A town that grows past that rectangle is still kept flat where it has buildings, since only bare ground, trees, bushes and flowers are raised, but its open grass is not.
- A drop is a one-way leg, so every ledge is a way into wherever it lands. `bun run verify stranded` (`app/verify/stranded.ts`) fails CI when a player can reach a cell and not get back to the start. Closing a glade in with trees beside a cliff, a wall walk or a roof makes one.
- A bridge is `wooden-floor` on the level of the ground it joins, three cells wide, with a `fence` on the two outer rows, and it runs one cell onto the ground at each end. `wooden-floor` is drawn short of its cell's edge, so a deck that stops at the gap leaves a strip of nothing between the planks and the ground. The ledge tiles connect to `wooden-floor` so the rim stops where the deck starts. A tree on ground one level below the deck is as tall as the gap and is drawn through the planks, so `raise:hills` clears the cover under a deck.
- `bakeSunSpans` (`app/lib/sunSpan.ts`) bakes a span for open air that is beside an occupied cell on its level, not only for occupied cells. The world shader filters the span texture, so the edge cell of a raised floor is blended with the air next to it, and air without a span reads as never lit.
- `mud`, `dirt` and `sand-ledge` draw their edges transparent where a neighbour does not connect to them, and whatever is under them in the stack shows through. With nothing there but autotiles they do not connect to (`dirt` under `mud`, or nothing at all) the edge is a black gap, so ground made of them goes on a full tile: `grass-2`, `dirt`, `mud`. `bun run verify content` warns about every such placement that draws an edge.
